# Landing-page content and proof guide

A conversion page should reduce the audience's uncertainty with verified
information. A long section list is not a strategy; include only content
that helps the intended visitor decide.

## Write the page brief

```text
Audience and context:
Problem they are trying to solve:
Offer and how it works:
Primary conversion action:
Traffic source / visitor intent:
Approved differentiators:
Available evidence:
Pricing, legal, or policy constraints:
Unknowns that need approval:
```

If audience, offer, or CTA is unclear, ask or label a limited assumption.
Do not invent a positioning statement and present it as approved.

## Map the reader's questions

Use the smallest sequence that answers the visitor's real questions:

1. Is this for someone like me?
2. What problem does it solve, and what will I receive?
3. How does it work in practice?
4. Why should I believe the claims?
5. What does it cost or require, if relevant?
6. What happens after I act?

An FAQ, comparison, pricing block, or demonstration earns its place only
when it answers an important question. Remove repeated sections that merely
restate benefits.

## Keep claims tied to proof

| Claim | Evidence source | Status | Safe presentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product capability | Current product or approved documentation | Verified / needs review | Describe actual behavior |
| Customer outcome | Approved, attributable evidence | Verified / unavailable | Quote with permission, or omit |
| Usage / performance metric | Defined source, cohort, period, method | Verified / unavailable | State context and limits |
| Certification / compliance | Current applicable record | Verified / unavailable | Name only what is approved |

Never manufacture testimonials, customer logos, metrics, awards, guarantees,
security claims, or certifications. If proof is unavailable, explain the
product mechanism honestly or omit the claim.

## Specify the action

For every CTA or form, verify:

- Label describes the next step (not a vague "Submit")
- Destination or action is implemented and accessible
- Any cost, commitment, or account requirement is clear
- Required fields and validation are explained
- Loading, success, failure, and duplicate-submit behavior are defined
- Privacy and consent language is accurate and approved

## Content and responsive review

Use real copy early enough to expose wrapping and hierarchy problems.
Preserve heading order; do not use heading levels for styling alone. Keep
meaningful copy in HTML, provide useful alternative text, and optimize
images without hiding essential information in them. At narrow widths,
check reading order and CTA access rather than merely shrinking columns.

## Review checklist

- [ ] The audience, offer, primary action, and visitor uncertainty are explicit.
- [ ] Each section answers a real question and supports the conversion goal.
- [ ] Every factual claim has an approved source or is removed / labeled.
- [ ] No fabricated testimonials, metrics, customers, or guarantees appear.
- [ ] CTA destinations, form states, semantics, and consent are correct.
- [ ] Mobile reading order, images, metadata, and performance are checked.
