# Image Art Direction

## When to use

Use when choosing photography, screenshots, illustrations, textures, or
other visual assets for an interface.

## When not to use

Do not add imagery solely to fill space or imply customers, product
capabilities, or outcomes that do not exist.

## Inputs and assumptions

Identify message, audience, brand, placement, crop behavior, source and
license, performance budget, and whether the asset is decorative or
informational.

## Workflow

1. State what the image contributes that text or layout does not.
2. Prefer authentic product assets and properly licensed originals.
3. Match composition, aspect ratio, crop, and focal point to actual
   responsive placements.
4. Provide concise useful alternative text for informative images; use an
   empty alternative for decorative images.
5. Use responsive sizes, efficient formats, and appropriate loading priority.
6. Record asset source, creator, license, attribution, and restrictions.
7. Review crop and contrast against real content and themes.

## Decision rules

- Never invent product screenshots, customer logos, or testimonials.
- Do not redistribute an asset when license terms are unclear.
- Avoid text embedded in images when the information must be searchable or
  accessible.
- Respect privacy and consent for identifiable people.

## Example

A product screenshot may use clearly labeled sample data, but must not be
presented as a real customer result or live production metric.

## Common failures

- Generic stock images unrelated to the product.
- Hero images that crop away the meaningful subject on mobile.
- Missing attribution, oversized files, or redundant alt text.

## Validation

Check authenticity, license record, responsive crop, alt-text purpose,
format/size, loading behavior, and visual contrast.
